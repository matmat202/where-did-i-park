    How it decides you parked

    A car stopping is not a car parking. The thing that separates a red light from a parking spot is that you get out and walk away. So the detector waits for all of this before it believes you:

    1. Sustained vehicular speed for at least 25 s — above 6.0 m/s (~21 km/h) reads as a car, 2.8–6.0 m/s as a bike. 2. Speed drops to walking or below, which opens a candidate at the last vehicular position. 3. You stay non-vehicular for 75 s and get at least 25 m from that spot. Only then is it recorded.

    Driving again before step 3 cancels the candidate, which is what rejects red lights and stop-and-go traffic. Sitting in the car for twenty minutes does not cancel it — the car is still where you stopped. What does cancel it is a gap in the data over 15 min: if fixes stop arriving we cannot promise the car did not move, so we admit that instead of guessing.

    Fixes with accuracy worse than 60 m are thrown away, and speed is median-smoothed over 5 samples so one GPS spike cannot fake a journey.

    Known limit: phone browsers suspend geolocation when you switch apps or lock the screen, so the page must stay open and in front for automatic detection to see the whole trip. If it misses one, Mark spot now always works.

    Positions are kept in this browser's local storage and are never uploaded.
